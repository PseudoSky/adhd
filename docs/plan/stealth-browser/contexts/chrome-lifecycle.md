## Goal

The Chrome lifecycle ('v3', backlog 19dfb641) built now by user decision (no later version will happen) on top of every feature and failsafe of the existing implementation (F1-F26 of EVAL-service-proxy.md): race-free launch, an idle reaper whose scope equals the source's, orphan reaping after `kill -9` through an out-of-process reaper, a file-backed circuit breaker, per-process presence records, exit-path shutdown and the isolated-context leak fixed at its source. The engine (`decideReap` and the tiers) is in-package behind two seams, `PresenceStore` and `ReaperLauncher`; the default launcher reuses `@adhd/sox-service-proxy`. Nothing the source does is removed.

## Acceptance criteria

Each criterion is a deterministic check in `docs/plan/stealth-browser/scripts/guard.py` (`python3 docs/plan/stealth-browser/scripts/guard.py chrome-lifecycle --only chrome-lifecycle.N`). None may be deferred. Tests written by this plan cite ticket `8468f7b1` and these ids ([inv:tests-cite]).

- [chrome-lifecycle.1] CL1 (F1 F2 F4) race-free launch: concurrent-launch.spec.ts runs 8 managers over one MemFs and proves exactly one spawn, the default host 127.0.0.1:9222, a CDP that never becomes ready within 10 s giving CHROME_UNREACHABLE_AFTER_LAUNCH, a loser waiting bounded for the winner, and a launch lock older than 15 s being reclaimed.
- [chrome-lifecycle.2] CL2 (F3 F17) self-heal and reset: relaunch.spec.ts proves that after another process kills the shared Chrome the next `ready()` relaunches, and that after a real shutdown a later `ready()` relaunches and a fresh idle timer fires again.
- [chrome-lifecycle.3] CL3 (F5) a fresh launch hides windows and the fast path does not re-hide (a window revealed for a human stays visible); hide-windows.spec.ts proves both.
- [chrome-lifecycle.4] CL4 (F6 F7) activity-bumped idle timer: idle-timer.spec.ts proves `bump()` re-arms (no fire at 299 ms, fire at 300 ms), the timer re-arms after it fired, `withBrowser` bumps when `fn` throws, and timers are unref'd.
- [chrome-lifecycle.5] CL5 (F8) cross-process coordination: cross-process.spec.ts proves process A defers when process B bumped within the idle window, reaps when B stayed silent past it, and ignores B when B is dead or its pid was reused (start-time token mismatch).
- [chrome-lifecycle.6] CL6 (F9) livelock protection: no-livelock.spec.ts proves two idle managers with zero http pages and history off never write presence while deferring (`bump()` is the only writer of `lastActivityAt`) and Chrome is reaped within idle plus grace.
- [chrome-lifecycle.7] CL7 (F10 F11 F12) page tier: page-tier.spec.ts proves an open http and an open https page pin Chrome, `chrome://newtab/`, `about:blank`, `devtools://` and `chrome-untrusted://` targets do not, and an unreachable CDP during the check proceeds.
- [chrome-lifecycle.8] CL8 (F13) history-recency tier: history-tier.spec.ts proves a visit within 2 minutes pins Chrome, `checkHistory: false` ignores history, a history database error proceeds, the default is on, and the tier is on when ANY live process enables it.
- [chrome-lifecycle.9] CL9 (F14 F15) shutdown modes: shutdown-modes.spec.ts proves `shutdown()` ignores every tier and `shutdownIfIdle()` defers to a sibling, to a page and to history.
- [chrome-lifecycle.10] CL10 (F16) ordered shutdown: ordered-shutdown.spec.ts proves all page targets are closed (allSettled) before Chrome is killed and that a failing close or kill is logged and the rest still runs.
- [chrome-lifecycle.11] CL11 (F18) group kill: group-kill.spec.ts proves SIGTERM to the process group, a bounded wait, then SIGKILL to the group, with a fallback to the single pid when the group signal fails (FakeProc log).
- [chrome-lifecycle.12] CL12 (F19) reaper scope: reaper-scope.spec.ts proves scope `port-occupant` (default) signals the port occupant (lsof, then fuser, with its pgid) when no owner is recorded and only the matching pid and start-time when an owner record exists, and scope `launched-only` never signals an unrecorded process.
- [chrome-lifecycle.13] CL13 (F20 F21 F22, gap G1) isolated pages: src/lib/lifecycle/isolated-page.ts exports openIsolatedPage and withIsolatedPage; `createIsolatedPageTarget` in cdp/targets.ts closes the browser CdpSession in a `finally` and disposes any created context when `Target.createBrowserContext` or `Target.createTarget` throws; isolated-page.spec.ts and cdp/targets.spec.ts prove no shared context, background by default, `reveal` unminimizes, `dispose` disposes the context, and a failure at createBrowserContext, createTarget, window bounds, connect or target-missing (`isolated-context-target-missing`) leaves `openCount()` at zero and no undisposed context, and `withIsolatedPage` disposes when `fn` throws.
- [chrome-lifecycle.14] CL14 (F23, gap G2) exit hooks: src/lib/lifecycle/exit-hooks.ts exports `installProcessExitHooks(manager, { forceExitMs = 5000, stdinEof, exit })` returning an uninstall function; exit-hooks.spec.ts proves each of exit, SIGINT, SIGTERM, uncaughtException, unhandledRejection and (opt-in) stdin end runs `release()` then the soft shutdown once, force-exits after 5000 ms, uses codes 130, 143 and 1, and uninstall removes the listeners (wiring into the hosts is surface-hosts.14).
- [chrome-lifecycle.15] CL15 (F24 N1 N5, gap G4) out-of-process reaper through the proxy launcher: src/lib/lifecycle/proxy-launcher.ts exports ServiceProxyReaperLauncher implementing `ReaperLauncher` (`ensure(cfg)` giving 'started' | 'already-running' | 'unavailable') over `ensureBackend` from @adhd/sox-service-proxy (imported statically, no dynamic import); reaper.spec.ts, with `vi.mock('@adhd/sox-service-proxy')`, proves the dispositions spawned, already-live and adopted-after-wait map to started or already-running and `failed` (including E_UDS_DIR_UNSAFE) maps to unavailable, a launcher giving unavailable leaves `ready()` successful with `status().reaper === false` and the in-process timers still apply, a reaper tick with all clients gone (kill -9: never released) reaps within TTL plus grace signalling only the recorded pid and start-time (none when the record is missing or mismatched), and the bound 'the next ready() of any process adopts or reaps' is tested as a rule; the reaper entry is `runChromeReaper(cfg)` and uses the same `decideReap` as the in-process path (one policy).
- [chrome-lifecycle.16] CL16 (N3, gap G4) presence: src/lib/lifecycle/presence.ts exports the `PresenceStore` / `PresenceHandle` interfaces and `FilePresenceStore` (one record file per process, atomic write, directory mode 0700 and own uid else PRESENCE_DIR_INSECURE, no file shared between processes, an unparsable file counts as dead); presence-eviction.spec.ts proves `list()` evicts dead, pid-reused (start-time mismatch) and TTL-expired records, `sweep()` returns the evicted, and a beating process survives.
- [chrome-lifecycle.17] CL17 (N4) launch circuit breaker: src/lib/lifecycle/launch-breaker.ts exports LaunchBreaker (file-backed, atomic rename; opens after 3 failures in 60 s and stays open 30 s); launch-breaker.spec.ts proves the 4th `ready()` throws LAUNCH_CIRCUIT_OPEN with `retryAfterMs` and no spawn, the breaker closes after the cool-off, success resets it and the state is shared across managers.
- [chrome-lifecycle.18] CL18 (F25) sibling diagnostic: sibling-warning.spec.ts proves `status().siblingWarning` is true at the threshold of live sibling records and that the diagnostic never kills anything.
- [chrome-lifecycle.19] CL19 (N2 N6) shared Chrome and grace: grace-exit.spec.ts proves N managers share one Chrome and that after the last `release()` Chrome exits within `shutdownGraceMs` (fake clock).
- [chrome-lifecycle.20] CL20 typed lifecycle errors: src/lib/lifecycle/errors.ts exports LifecycleError with exactly the seven codes LAUNCH_FAILED, LAUNCH_LOCK_TIMEOUT, CHROME_UNREACHABLE_AFTER_LAUNCH, LAUNCH_CIRCUIT_OPEN, PRESENCE_DIR_INSECURE, REAPER_UNAVAILABLE and ISOLATED_CONTEXT_FAILED; errors.spec.ts reaches each with `cause` or `detail` (`retryAfterMs`, `port`, `path`) and a one-line message naming the fix.
- [chrome-lifecycle.21] CL21 (F26) best-effort I/O: best-effort.spec.ts proves presence and ownership write failures never fail a call.
- [chrome-lifecycle.22] CL22 seam contract: src/test/unit/lifecycle/presence-contract.ts exports a contract suite that presence-contract.spec.ts runs against `FilePresenceStore`; `PresenceStore` and `ReaperLauncher` are the only two seams (decideReap, the tiers, the breaker, the isolated pages and the exit hooks never import the proxy package or a concrete store), so a proxy-backed presence store later passes the same suite and needs no change to the policy engine; a fake `ReaperLauncher` is substitutable in the manager tests.
